# Sound banks: choosing, checking and configuring recordings

The default MS Basic bank renders every General MIDI program but with few samples per instrument. Its picked bass puts about 2% of its energy on the fundamental at D2, most guitars and strings change only level (not timbre) with velocity, and the "Expr." presets need CC2. For a presentable result, give the important parts dedicated recordings and keep MS Basic for the rest.

## Vetted banks (checked 2026-11-02)

Twenty-nine optional banks: five published as SF2 by FreePats and twenty-four built here from sample libraries. `fetch_soundbanks.py` downloads all twenty-nine from the repository's release: the twenty-four as this project built them (an xz-compressed SF2 each, checked against the same locked hashes a build gives) and the five repacked unchanged as tar.xz (checked against the hashes of FreePats' own files). With `--from-source`, or where the release cannot be reached, it builds the twenty-four from the samples and takes the five from FreePats.

Every bank here is CC0 1.0 except the four Iowa banks, whose publisher states that the recordings "may be downloaded and used for any projects, without restrictions", and the steel drum, which its author dedicated to the public domain under the Unlicense; music rendered with any of them needs no credit. Licences were read on the publisher pages and in the bundled LICENSE/README. GitHub release digests were matched where GitHub publishes them; otherwise the downloaded archive hash is recorded here. A hash is integrity, not an antivirus result.

### Dynamic layers and legato presets

The sustained presets below also come in two more forms on the same program, for `performance.dynamics` and crossfade legato ([schema.md](schema.md), [orchestration.md](orchestration.md)):

- **Dynamic layers**, bank 16 + k: the k-th recorded dynamic alone, from the softest (k = 0), deaf to velocity. A layer with fewer recorded dynamics than its preset's richest maps onto the same scale, so one key range may repeat a dynamic.
- **Legato**, bank 32 + k: the same takes entered past their attack, at the first 10 ms frame within 2 dB of the take's steady level plus 20 ms (at most 0.6 s), faded in over 10 ms.

Only the preset at bank 0 of each program listed has them: another preset on the same program (Contrabass Straight at 1:43, a spiccato, a pizzicato) plays its velocity layers alone. `review --config` names a part that asks for dynamics or crossfade legato on such a preset, with the programs that have them, and a render stops on it before anything is synthesized.

FluidSynth applies a zone's attenuation (SF2 generator 48) at 0.4 of its centibels, as the EMU8000 did, so `soundbank.py` writes 2.5 times the centibels meant to be heard (a dynamic borrowed from a louder take 12 dB down is written as 300), and `musiclib/layered.py` reads it the same way; the loop search sizes its window from each recording's own pitch. Seven banks were rebuilt for these on 3 November: `tuba_war` b2, `vsco_colors` b2, `vcsl_winds` b2, `iowa_brass` b2, `iowa_winds` b4, `iowa_strings` b4 and `iowa_perc` b3.

`scripts/fetch_soundbanks.py` downloads, verifies and configures all of these (`--only` picks some). The Iowa banks fetch each recording from the University of Iowa site, check its size and SHA-256, cut every note where the bank's notes manifest records it, check each note's hash and must reproduce the locked output hash; `iowa_strings` alone fetches 1.17 GB. Archives contain only the bank, LICENSE, README and an image. Built banks fetch their samples one by one from the pinned commit, check each against its git blob hash and must reproduce the locked output hash; the kit's FLAC files are decoded with the configured FFmpeg. It lists members before extracting and rejects absolute paths, `..` and links. Only when the release cannot be reached are the FreePats banks unpacked from their own .7z archives, which need a libarchive tar with LZMA (Windows 11's own `tar.exe`, macOS `tar`, or `bsdtar` on Linux: `libarchive-tools`) or else 7-Zip's `7z`; the `tar.exe` of Windows 10 cannot read them, so there install 7-Zip. Downloads that end short or do not match the lock are tried again and never kept, and a bank that fails does not stop the others. Keep archives and unpacked banks outside the skill folder, for example in a `.runtime` folder next to your projects.

## Measured behaviour (probe, not listening)

Numbers come from raw synthesis of a probe project: velocity sweeps, the intended figures and the needed registers on every bank.

- Attack from note-on to −12 dB of the note's peak: guitars 1–3 ms, basses 0–3 ms, kit 3.4 ms median, piano 2.5 ms, brass 13–17 ms, VSCO violin spiccato about 55 ms, VSCO sustained violins about 400 ms (a bowed swell). Section strings therefore need `offset_ms` (≈ −90 for sustains, −35 to −40 for spiccato) and cannot articulate fast lines from sustain samples: route short notes to spiccato and long notes to sustain as separate tracks.
- FSBS guitars map each velocity value to its own sample pair and Big Rusty Drums alternates its round robins on neighbouring velocities, so ordinary velocity variation also varies the recording. A drum part steps through a kit's round robins on repeated hits by itself (`performance.round_robin`); a pitched part at one velocity still repeats one sample.
- Big Rusty Drums keeps the recorded dynamics: velocity 20 sits about 17 dB under 127 on kick and snare (a kit normalized hit by hit may differ by only 3–7 dB), so write velocities as a drummer plays, ghost notes soft and accents hard. Closed and pedal hats choke an open one. The cymbals are 6 dB above the library's default program; a crash takes about 8 s to fall 60 dB, so leave `tail_seconds` of 5–6 after a final crash. A 100 s release makes short notes one-shots.
- VCSL Steinway B has three velocity layers normalized to the same peak (the library asks for a velocity curve instead of layer gains): the level comes from the synthesizer's velocity curve, about −12 dB at velocity 64, and the timbre brightens at velocities 65 and 97. Its close pair measures left/right correlation about 0.4.
- Bass YR has one velocity layer and samples only up to B♭2/A2; keep octave jumps inside the sampled keys. Its fundamental is stronger than MS Basic's, but a near-sine additive sub that follows the bass line still helps small speakers.
- VSCO soft string layers were recorded 14–23 dB below the loud ones (viola most); build b2 sets them 9 dB down (`layer_levels_db`), brass in 3–4 steps, so a soft passage keeps the pp timbre and the sections in one patch match.
- VSCO woodwinds, tremolo and pizzicato (build b3):
  - **Length.** Sustained winds and tremolo hold at least 8 s. Staccato samples last about 0.5 s and pizzicato about 1 s, whatever the written length.
  - **Attack** to −12 dB: pizzicato 12 ms, oboe and bassoon 20–24 ms, flute 56 ms, clarinet and tremolo about 100 ms. Give clarinet and tremolo parts `offset_ms` around −60 and the flute about −35.
  - **Levels.** Soft layers sit 9 dB under the loud one (4.5 and 9 dB with three layers), on top of the synthesizer's velocity curve. Velocity 50 lands 22–29 dB under 120, and 85 about 6–13 dB under. The flute's low octave is 12 dB weaker than its middle, as on the instrument.
  - **Gaps.** Staccato flute samples start at A4; below that the A4 sample is stretched. The library's top clarinet sample (F♯6) is a semitone flat and is left out, so D6 serves up to E6.
- VCSL harp, mallets and bells (build b2):
  - **Attack** to −12 dB is 2–13 ms.
  - **Decay.** Build b4 keeps each take of the harp, tubular bells, timpani and Steinway until its ring falls 60 dB under its peak, at most 30 s (the timpani 12 s): low harp notes ring up to about 25 s, the bells and the low piano past 30 s. Build b3 had cut them at 8-14 s while they were still 27-46 dB down. Marimba (about 1.4 s), xylophone (0.7 s) and vibraphone end on their own.
  - **Levels.** The harp's two layers were normalized in the library, so the synthesizer's velocity curve alone shapes them. The mallets and bells were recorded 14–27 dB apart between layers; the build sets them 4–8 dB apart (`layer_levels_db`). With the velocity curve, velocity 50 lands about 21 dB under 120 and 85 about 6–10 dB under, as on the other banks.
  - **Sparse sampling.** Marimba and xylophone are sampled about every fifth to seventh semitone, so each sample is stretched up to about 3.5 semitones. The harp's lowest sample is E1.
- VSCO string sections (`vsco_strings`, build b1): VSCO 2 CE samples the cello section from C2 to F5, the violas from C3 to D6 and the violins from G3 to D6, every third or fourth semitone. The `vsco` presets combine them by register (cellos to F♯3, violas to E4, violins above) and stretch the violins' D6 take up to G7; `vsco_strings` gives each section its own preset over its whole range, mapped at most 4 semitones past its top sample. Write each section part on its own preset so its tone stays that section's; above F♯6 the violins have no sample, so give the top octave to flutes, piccolo or the solo violin (sampled to C7). `review --config` lists any note played more than 4 semitones from its sample.
- VSCO solo violin and trumpet (build b4):
  - **Attack** to −12 dB: violin about 20 ms, trumpet about 11 ms. They speak quickly enough for melodies without `offset_ms`.
  - **Length.** The violin and the straight trumpet hold at least 8 s. The trumpet's vibrato samples last about 4.7 s, so hold longer notes on the straight preset. Violin spiccato lasts about 0.5 s and trumpet staccato about 0.8 s.
  - **Sampling.** The violin is sampled about every third, the trumpet every two to four semitones. Use `performance.legato` for a sung line.
- VCSL timpani (build b3):
  - **Pitch.** VCSL names its timpani takes by drum and tuning letter, not by pitch, so the build measured each take's principal tone: the (1,1) mode, whose partners (2,1) to (5,1) sit near 1.5, 2, 2.4 and 2.9 times it (Rossing). It then mapped one position per key from D2 to B3 and corrected the rest by fine tune.
  - FreePats' independent timpani, assembled by ear from a few of the same takes, sits on average about half a semitone lower on the positions both use. Listen against a tuned instrument before a timpani part carries the harmony.
  - **Response.** Attack to −12 dB about 6 ms. A hit rings about 4.4 s above −40 dB of its peak; samples end at 6 s, and a release lasts 2 s. Velocity 50 lands about 28 dB under 120, and 85 about 10 dB under.
- VCSL orchestra kit (build b3):
  - **Balance.** The kit keeps the recorded balance between pieces, except the small percussion (triangle, tambourine, sleigh bells, mark tree), raised 6–10 dB so it carries in a band mix. At velocity 110, the bass drum, snare and clash cymbals peak about 20–24 dB under full scale and the small percussion about 27–32 dB under. For a part that needs its own level, give it its own track with the same patch.
  - **Timed swells.** The suspended cymbal swells peak about 1.2 s (key 27), 1.5 s (28), 3.7 s (29) and 7.3 s (30) after note-on: start the note that long before the downbeat it should land on.
  - **Glissandi.** The mark tree glissandi peak about 0.3 s (84), 1.3 s (85) and 2.5 s (86) after note-on.
  - **Rolls and hits.** Rolls (40, 59) stop at note-off with a short release. Every other piece is a one-shot that rings out whatever the note length.
  - **Ring.** The clash cymbals and the gong ring for most of their 12 and 15 s samples. After a final clash, set `tail_seconds` to 12, or the render's `tail_quiet` check fails.
- Black And Blue Basses darkblack (build b1):
  - **Octave.** The library names notes an octave above standard pitch: its b1 is the low B string, B0.
  - **Tuning.** The build fine-tunes the regular notes from their measured pitch, typically by 0–20 cents. The ghost and staccato notes are too short to measure and are left as recorded.
  - **Velocity.** Velocities 1–24 play ghost notes, about 28–32 dB under velocity 120. The regular notes span 25–127: velocity 50 lands about 29 dB under 120, and 85 about 13 dB under. Write ordinary notes from about 40 up, so they never fall into the ghost range.
  - **Length.** Regular notes ring up to 5 s, staccato notes about 0.3 s.
- Osiris Piano (build b3):
  - **Pedal.** The library recorded every note twice, with the dampers down and with the sustain pedal held, so the strings ring in sympathy. A SoundFont cannot switch recordings with the pedal, so choose the preset. On the pedal preset, hold notes through a harmony with CC64 in `performance.cc` and lift it at the next harmony (`[beat, 64, 0]`, then `[beat + 0.05, 64, 127]`), or write the durations long.
  - **Attack.** The library keeps about 25 ms of key noise before each hammer strike. The build trims it to a few milliseconds, so the tone starts about 5 ms after note-on, in time with the other banks.
  - **Clicks.** Many pedal takes, most of them soft, recorded a short knock from the piano's action while the note rang on, most likely the key let go under the held pedal: 0.2–3.8 s after the strike, broadband from about 1 to 12 kHz and up to 24 dB over the ring in 3–12 kHz. Every note played on such a take knocked at the same moment, heard most under quiet pedalled playing. Build b3 repairs them (`declick`): 36 pedal takes and 2 pedal-up takes, a few of them for a later knock 5–14 s in. Of the 207 stereo takes, 174 are bit for bit those of b2; the others differ only in the repaired 0.1 s or so, and their level by at most 0.02 dB.
  - **Velocity.** Two layers split at 64, levelled to the same early RMS, so the synthesizer's velocity curve sets loudness (velocity 64 about 12 dB under 127). On some notes the two layers still differ by 2–5 dB across 63/64: keep a passage that must be even on one side of 64.
  - **Damping.** The release follows the register: 1.0 s in the bass, 0.65 s in the middle, 0.42 s in the treble. From F♯6 up there are no dampers, so those notes ring out.
  - **Length and tuning.** Each take rings until it falls 60 dB under its peak: up to 17.6 s with the dampers down and 20.5 s on the pedal preset; from C7 up, 1.1–3 s and 3.8–6.9 s. The tuning is kept as recorded: the treble is stretched up to about 25 cents sharp, as pianos are tuned.
  - **Level.** The bank sits about 6 dB under the others, so no take is limited at full scale. Track levels in the mix make up the difference.
- Karoryfer x bigcat cello (build b1):
  - **Sustain.** Each take loops, so a held note lasts as long as the key. Some takes sagged at their loop end, and a held note pulsed on every repeat. Measured over a 14 s hold, the deepest dip under the median level was 4.1 dB in the median take (14.5 dB at worst). Where a take sagged, the build shortened the loop and crossfaded it, which brought the dips to 2.5 dB (9.1 dB at worst). What remains is the bow's own movement.
  - **Vibrato.** The takes are straight; the library adds vibrato with an LFO. The build adds 5.5 Hz at ±12 cents, starting 0.35 s into the note, so short notes stay straight. CC1 deepens it by up to 50 cents.
  - **Velocity.** Four recorded dynamics on the library's split (p 1–32, mp 33–64, mf 65–96, f 97–127) are levelled to one median under a straight velocity line. Velocity 10 sits about 28 dB under 127, and every layer boundary moves the level by under 2.5 dB, so velocity sets loudness and the recorded dynamic sets the tone.
  - **Attack and range.** Attack to −20 dB of the peak is 5–14 ms, and the release is 0.4 s. At velocity 90 the early RMS falls from about −38 dB in the low register to −44 dB at the top.
  - **Short notes.** Staccato lasts 0.5–1 s. Pizzicato rings 1–6 s, the open C longest.
- VSCO extra: trombone, tuba, double bass and piccolo (build b1):
  - **Holding.** The library's sustained takes have no loops, and its loud brass takes last only 2–4 s. The build cuts a loop from each take's steady part (`loop: "found"`), at least 1.2 s long, so a held note lasts as long as the key.   - **Bow changes.** The double bass's vibrato takes keep the player's bow changes, which recur about every 2.3 s as dips of 4–6 dB, in the recording and in the loop. The straight takes hold within about 3 dB, so use the straight preset for long quiet notes.
  - **Attack** to −20 dB of the peak: trombone 14–60 ms (about 100 ms on its lowest E2), tuba 18–41 ms, double bass 9–42 ms, piccolo 13–88 ms.
  - **Levels.** The recorded dynamics are set 3 dB apart on the trombone (five of them), 6 dB on the tuba (three) and 9 dB on the double bass (two, split at velocity 85: keep a passage that must stay even on one side of it). With the synthesizer's velocity curve, velocity 50 lands 24–27 dB under 120, and 85 about 6–12 dB under. The piccolo has one dynamic, so the curve alone shapes it: 50 lands about 15 dB under 120.
  - **Sampling.** Trombone every two semitones, tuba every four, double bass every two to three; the piccolo has five notes, each stretched over about seven semitones.
  - **Short notes.** Trombone short, tuba staccato and double bass spiccato last about 0.3 s, pizzicato about 0.5 s and piccolo staccato about 0.2 s, whatever the written length.
- VSCO colours and VCSL keys, winds and world (build b1):
  - **Octaves.** VCSL names most of these an octave under sounding pitch (the Renaissance organ's 4′ two octaves); every build checks each take's pitch and sets the octave, and takes its key range from the notes found (`keys: "auto"`).
  - **Tuning.** The kalimbas and the mbira are tuned their own way, about +15 to +55 cents from equal temperament on the Tanzanian kalimba; each take is fine-tuned to the nearest semitone where its pitch could be measured (up to 60 cents). Some tines' partials defeat the pitch check and keep their recorded tuning: listen before such an instrument carries a melody.
  - **Harpsichords** keep one recorded strength, as the instrument does: velocity changes nothing, so shape a line by touch and timing. The Flemish 8′+4′ preset sounds both registers together, as coupled manuals do.
  - **The TX81Z** voices were recorded from the synthesizer, so they hold steady and loop cleanly; the FM piano and clavinet sit in a band mix the way a 1980s keyboard does.
  - **Sizes.** `vcsl_keys` is the largest bank here (799 MB, the Kawai grand alone about 390 MB); fetch it with `--only vcsl_keys` when a part needs it.
  - **Knocks.** The VCSL Steinway (`vcsl` 0:0), Kawai and uprights (`vcsl_keys` 0:1, 0:3, 1:3) and the VSCO upright (`vsco_colors` 0:0) recorded short knocks in some takes while the note rang on, as the Osiris pedal takes did, but later: a few in the first 2 s, most of them 3–20 s after the strike (12 Steinway takes, 101 Kawai and upright takes, 31 VSCO upright takes), where only a long or pedalled note reaches them; the demos reach one, in the near-silent tail of Rain on the Tram Line. Their builds keep them as recorded. A project that holds such notes can build the bank locally with `declick` on the piano's layer (`soundbank.py build`, a new output path), which repairs them as on the Osiris.
  - **Noise and dynamics.** Under a ringing note the soft takes of these pianos carry the recording's rumble and hiss, which a pedal adds up over many notes. Measured on the softest layer, rumble (20–60 Hz) and hiss (8–16 kHz) over each take's second half under the note's early level: the Steinway about 32 and 60 dB, the Kawai 30 and 47 (10 and 31 on its noisiest takes), the Yamaha upright 73 and 55, the Knight upright 54 and 68, the VSCO upright 33 and 51, where the Osiris pedal takes keep 58 and 73. In a mix other parts and the reverb cover it; it shows in quiet, pedalled solo piano, so choose `piano_osiris` there, or the Yamaha upright for an upright's sound. The builds keep the noise as recorded: a noise reduction measured on each take was tried and set aside, because what it left of the noise rose and fell with the playing. The Kawai and the uprights were recorded with their velocity layers 14–18 dB apart, and with the velocity curve on top a note at velocity 50 sounded 25–44 dB under one at 126 (16 dB on the Osiris and the Steinway) and a layer split jumped 5–20 dB. `vcsl_keys` b2 and `vsco_colors` b3 level every dynamic and round robin of a note to one level (`match_notes: "across"`, `layer_levels_db` 0): measured dry on ten pitches, velocity 50 now sits 16 dB under 126 on all six pianos, a split moves a note's first 0.4 s by 2.2 dB at most (the Steinway 2.4, the Osiris 10.6 on one note), and neighbouring keys stay within 3–4 dB (95th percentile).
- University of Iowa Musical Instrument Samples (`iowa_brass` build b1, `iowa_perc` build b2, `iowa_winds` and `iowa_strings` build b3):
  - **Recordings.** Each file holds one dynamic of a chromatic run (a scale played one note at a time, a few seconds per note), recorded in an anechoic chamber 5 feet from the player: dry, so the render's spaces supply the room. The winds and brass were recorded 1997–1998 in mono at 16 bit, the percussion in 2013 in stereo at 24 bit.
  - **Intake.** `soundbank.py split` found every note: a YIN pitch every 10 ms, searched only around the file's named run, cut into candidate notes at silences and at pitch steps of 1–3 semitones, then matched to the named run in order by dynamic programming. A note played twice keeps its better-tuned take, a breath or a key noise is passed over, and an octave the pitch tracker slipped to still matches. 1,890 notes came from 485 files. The string players skipped some notes on some strings; those notes come from another string, or from a neighbouring dynamic. Build b2 re-cut 82 notes that b1 had taken from a faint ring or the room's hum, or started in a plucked note's ring after its attack: 38 cello and 24 double-bass pizzicati, 10 cello arco notes, a violin pizzicato and 9 bells.
  - **Tuning.** The players were often off: the horn up to 55 cents on its pedal notes, the E-flat clarinet and the flutes up to 60, the violin, viola and cello by 20 cents in the median. Each note is fine-tuned from the pitch measured over its whole steady part (`pitch_source: "split"`), up to a semitone.
  - **Holding.** Notes last 2–3 s (the violin 5.5 s), so every sustained note loops on its steady part, with the loop end searched over the last 0.3 s for the best match over at least one period, and over 0.12 s for the strings so a vibrato's phase lines up too. The median loop match is 0.99 on horn, trombone and clarinets, 0.98 on the flutes (their breath lowers it) and 0.6–0.94 on the strings over the longer window; the strings' seams cross over in 0.3 s.
  - **Levels.** pp, mf and ff are set 12 and 6 dB under the loudest on the brass, 9 and 4.5 dB on winds and strings, 12 and 6 dB on the percussion. A missing dynamic borrows the nearest recorded one at the level of the dynamic it serves (`borrowed_level: "served"`), so the horn's low register, recorded only at ff, still enters softly.
  - **Noise.** The publisher gated many files: the brass files, a sixth to a quarter of each string file and a fifth of the bass clarinet's are digital silence between notes, and the E-flat clarinet's leave only about 0.15 s of room sound before each note. The flutes keep their breath under the notes, about 33–57 dB under the loudest frame. Measured away from the notes, the steady noise is about −59 dBFS on the cello and −65 on the double bass (mostly rumble under 100 Hz), −81 on the violin, −86 to −90 on the viola, −61 on the flutes and −66 to −70 on the clarinets; in the hiss band, 4–16 kHz, about −82 dBFS on the flutes, −87 to −98 on the clarinets and strings and −101 on the crotales and Thai gongs. Build b2 reduces it on every take of the strings and winds by up to 12 dB and on the bells, crotales and Thai gongs by up to 10 dB; the cymbals and tam-tams, whose sound is itself noise-like, stay as recorded. The cello and double-bass rumble, and the noise between the double-bass notes, which peaks at the open strings' pitches, can be as loud as the fundamental of a soft low note: b2 took up to 8 dB from the fundamental of a pp double-bass E2, so build b3 leaves each string and wind note's own partials under 300 Hz as recorded (`keep_partials_below`). Measured over every take of the two banks, a note's fundamental now moves by at most 0.4 dB and its first five partials by at most 1 dB, and its level while it sounds by 0.04 dB or less in the median; what the reduction takes is the rumble under each note, the hiss over the softest takes' top octaves and the noise in the tails. The clarinets' own reed and air noise is another matter: it reaches 20 kHz, starts and stops with each tone and grows toward ff (in 8–20 kHz, 15–20 dB over the recording's noise just before the note), so the denoiser leaves it. In an exposed line it can read as hiss; in the woodwinds probe one take per note on the E-flat clarinet (`dynamics_mode: "take"`) and a `lowpass` of 11 kHz on it (10 kHz on the bass clarinet) brought it to the flutes' level above 6 kHz. A pp note raised to its layer level still brings what is left up with it: hear a soft passage before it carries a mix.
- VCSL pipe organ (build b2):
  - **Velocity** changes nothing, as on an organ. Shape swells with CC11.
  - **Speech.** The pipes take 20–40 ms to speak, and the soft stop's bass up to about 90 ms. Give an organ part a small negative `offset_ms` if it must lock to a beat.
  - **Holding.** Each key was held about 10 s, with no loop. Build b2 loops each recording between 2 and 9.6 s after its onset, clear of key and action noises, so a note holds as long as the key and stops within about 0.35 s of its release; the room carries the decay. The full registration beats slowly between its ranks, at middle C about once every 1.2 s by 5–8 dB, as in the recording.
  - **Voicing.** The full registration keeps its recorded balance, about 10 dB more early RMS in the bass than at the top. The soft stop's notes differed by 5–14 dB from their neighbours, and the build pulls each toward them (at most 6 dB). That leaves steps of up to about 5 dB.
  - **Pitch.** Both manuals sound an octave above their file names (8′ pitch), and the full registration adds a quieter sub-octave from about middle C up. The pedal sounds at its file names (its 16′ fundamental).
- The CC0 additions (`bass_sneaky`, `guitar_shiny`, `erhu`, `steel_drum`, `tuba_war`, `sax_bear`, `vocal_ah`, build b1):
  - **Tuning.** On low plucks, high erhu notes, the steel pan and the sax, the pitch tracker sometimes reads the second harmonic or the octave below. These specs set `fold_octaves`, so such a reading counts as the named note that many cents out, and the offset becomes fineTune within the layer's limit (45 cents; 60 for the sax; 70 for the bass and the voice). Measured that way, the bass's plucked low E sat about 60 cents flat, the sax 19 cents sharp in the median and up to 60, the voice up to 64 cents flat. Left as recorded: two bass takes with no readable pitch (a muted thump and one pluck) and the steel pan's upper notes, whose tracker reading lands a twelfth below.
  - **Steel drum.** Mapped as its author's SFZ maps it: C4–B4 from his 2021 re-recording at four strengths, C5–B5 from the first session at three, and the hardest strokes (velocity 111–127) from the first session in both octaves.
  - **Erhu.** The sustained and sul tasto takes last 2.3–3.4 s with an irregular vibrato, so no loop end matches closely (median 0.73): the loops cross over in 0.35 s at equal power, and a long held note repeats its last second or so. Prefer notes under about 2 s, and the Short preset for detached playing.
  - **Baritone sax.** The sustained preset plays the library's looped p and f takes, so a held note lasts as long as the key; the short preset holds its marcato and staccato takes, about 0.5 s each. Growls, sub-tone and key noises are not built.
  - **Voice.** Only the sustained vowel: the library's short notes slide up into their pitch (by up to 1.5 semitones within 0.3 s) or land beside it, so they are left out.
  - **Downloads.** Each lock entry names only the files its spec uses (folders and globs such as `Samples/*_looped_[pf].wav`), so the sax fetches 514 of the library's 757 files and the voice 25 of 117.
- VCSL winds (build b1): the tenor saxophone's two channels are partly out of phase: a rendered tenor line measured a left/right correlation of −0.17 at full width, so in mono it thins out. Give a tenor part `mix.width` near 0.2, as a close-miked soloist would be.

## Configuring

```text
python <skill>/scripts/soundbank.py inspect <bank.sf2>
python <skill>/scripts/soundbank.py build <skill>/references/soundbanks/vsco2ce_orchestra.json --root <VSCO-2-CE checkout> --output <runtime>/vsco-b3.sf2 --receipt <project>/vsco_build_receipt.json
python <skill>/scripts/studio.py add-soundfont --config local-config.json --name grand --path <bank.sf2> --license "CC0-1.0: ..." --source <url> --version <version and hash> --output local-config.studio.json
python <skill>/scripts/studio.py doctor --config local-config.studio.json
```

`add-soundfont` always writes a new configuration; `doctor` re-hashes every bank. In a project, set the track's `soundfont`, `patch` (most single-instrument banks use bank 0 program 0) and, for a kit that is not on GM keys, a `key_map` from GM numbers to its keys. Big Rusty Drums needs none: it is on GM keys (35–53, 57 and 59; 48 and 50 replay the 14-inch tom two and four semitones up).

## Building a bank from a sample set

`soundbank.py build` reads a JSON spec (the ones in `references/soundbanks/` are worked examples) and writes an SF2 plus a receipt with every source file's hash and every decision. A spec has `name`, `comment`, `copyright`, `target_rms` (0.08) and `presets`; a preset has `name`, `program`, optional `bank`, and `layers`, each layer becoming one instrument.

| Layer field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `dir`, `pattern` | Folder and a regex over file names with named groups `note` (C4, F#2), `dyn` and `rr`; `.wav`, `.flac` (through `--ffmpeg`) and uncompressed `.aif`/`.aiff` files are read. |
| `mics` | Instead of `dir`: microphones of the same takes, `[{"dir", "gain", "pan", "name"}]`, the same file name in each folder or, with `name` (`["MicA", "MicB"]`), one that differs by the microphone's tag. They are summed sample-aligned; a mono one is placed with a constant-power `pan` (−1 to 1). |
| `dynamics`, `velocity` | The `dyn` values used, loudest last, and the velocity range each serves. A note missing a dynamic borrows the nearest one. |
| `keys`, `octave_offset`, `root` | Playable key range; a shift for libraries that number octaves differently; a fixed root key for unpitched layers with no `note` in the name. |
| `pitches` | For takes named by position rather than pitch: a map from the regex group `id` to a measured MIDI pitch with cents (`{"1C": 39.95}`). The root is the nearest key and the rest becomes fine tune; positions left out are not used. |
| `pitch_check`, `fmin`, `fmax`, `yin_threshold`, `max_correction_cents` | Pitch measured against the name: a whole-octave median error is fixed, smaller ones become fineTune. Turn it off for drums. |
| `fold_octaves` | A single take read an octave or two from its name, give or take a few cents (the tracker locked onto a harmonic or a subharmonic), counts as the named note that many cents out. |
| `denoise` | `{"reduce_db": 12, "keep_partials_below": 300}`: the steady hiss and rumble of the recordings reduced by up to that much, by a short-time Wiener gain with a decision-directed SNR estimate (Ephraim and Malah 1984; Scalart and Filho 1996); the gain is shared by both channels and never goes under the limit, so a note keeps its level and tone and the residual noise does not flicker. With `keep_partials_below` (Hz) each take's own partials under that frequency stay as recorded (the bins within the window's main lobe or 3 % of each harmonic of its measured pitch): a soft low note can be no louder than a recording's rumble, where the gain alone would thin it. The noise each take is measured against: for notes `split` cut, their recording's own, stored in the manifest (frames away from every note, the margins narrowing for recordings with short pauses, skipping a publisher's gated digital silence, leaving out frames louder than the quiet ones such as a breath or a last ring, each band the median of its bins so a hum line or a partial does not count); a recording with no usable pause borrows the median of its layer's recordings at the same dynamic; other takes use their quiet frames pooled per dynamic. The receipt names each take's source (`noise_measured_from`). For takes with real noise between their notes; not for breath or bow noise that belongs to the sound, nor for cymbals and tam-tams, whose sound is itself noise-like. Takes recorded one note per file have no pause to measure: their quiet frames are the takes' own last ring wherever a take was cut before its note died away, and a gain measured on them takes from soft attacks (on a soft VSCO violin take, 2.9 dB under 300 Hz and 4.2 dB over 4 kHz in the first 80 ms); find plain noise in such takes first. [noise.md](noise.md) gives every bank's measured noise: none calls for more reduction. |
| `declick` | `{}` (or the options of `find_clicks`): mechanical clicks in a take that rings on, such as the knock of a key let go under the sustain pedal, found and repaired after the onset trim, before the ring cut is measured. A click is broadband and stands alone: in its frames (1024 samples every 128) at least 40 % of the bins from 2 to 12 kHz rise more than 6 dB over their level 50–120 ms either side, its 3–12 kHz band stands 6 dB over the frames 20–110 ms either side, nothing else within 0.25 s rises within 4 dB of it, and it lasts at most 0.15 s. A low string's own pulse, once a period, and a soft take's flickering hammer noise rise again and again, so they stay as recorded. In the frames that reach into a click, each bin is held to its level interpolated between the four frames just before and just after it, plus 1.5 dB, with its phase kept: a ringing partial holds its level across the click and passes, the click's added noise does not, and samples outside those frames stay bit for bit. The receipt lists each repair (`declicked_ms`: start, end and the 3–12 kHz rise in dB, from the onset). It is for takes recorded one note per file: a second strike's hammer noise can read as a click too, and its new note's first frames would be lowered, so on recordings holding several notes check the receipt's spans first. Clicks are read on the average of the channels, so one recorded in opposite polarity on the two can be missed. |
| `keys: "auto"`, `key_margin` | The key range from the notes found, after the octave check, plus `key_margin` (2) semitones each side. |
| `pitch_source` | `"split"`: take each note's pitch from the `split.json` beside notes cut by `soundbank.py split`, measured over the note's whole steady part, instead of a 0.6 s excerpt. |
| `borrowed_level` | `"served"`: a note missing a dynamic borrows the nearest louder one at the level `layer_levels_db` gives the dynamic it serves (a zone attenuation), not at its own. |
| `level`, `match_notes`, `match_limit_db`, `layer_levels_db`, `gain` | `"peak"` matches percussive layers by peak instead of early RMS; notes are pulled toward their layer's median unless `match_notes` is false, or with `"local"` toward the median of the note and its two nearest sampled neighbours each side, which evens out uneven takes but keeps the register's slope, or with `"across"` every dynamic and round robin of a note to one level on a register slope the dynamics share (the median of each one's local level against its own median), so a velocity split changes the tone and not the level, which the velocity curve sets; `match_limit_db` caps the pull (default 6 dB; more also raises a soft take's noise); `layer_levels_db` sets recorded dynamics relative to the loudest. |
| `loop`, `loop_window`, `loop_repair` | `"recorded"` plays each take's own sustain loop from its WAV `smpl` chunk, so a held note lasts as long as the key. `"found"` cuts a loop from a take recorded without one, inside `loop_window` (seconds after the onset, default `[0.8, 600]`): the longest stretch clear of noises (frames more than `loop_repair.noise_db`, default 4 dB, over the median of their own half second; raise it for tremolo), at least 0.8 s. `loop_repair` (`{"sag_db": 1.5, "crossfade": 0.12}`) moves a loop end that sags back to where the level is steady, matches it in phase to the loop start and crossfades into it; found loops always get it. `period_search: true` compares at least one period of the note (low brass) and searches the end over the last 0.3 s of the steady part; `match_seconds` lengthens the comparison (0.12 lines up a vibrato), `min_loop` lowers the 0.8 s minimum, and a take too short for its window loops from earlier, never inside its first 0.15 s. |
| `dynamic_layers`, `legato` (preset fields) | `{"bank": 16}` adds one velocity-deaf preset per recorded dynamic at that bank and up; `{"bank": 32, "attack": 0.01}` adds legato versions of those, each zone starting past its take's attack (`startAddrsOffset`) and fading in over `attack` seconds. The takes are shared with the main preset, so the bank grows only by zones. |
| `vibrato` | `{"rate_hz", "depth_cents", "delay_seconds"}` for takes recorded straight: the synthesizer's vibrato LFO, starting after the delay so short notes stay straight. The mod wheel (CC1) adds depth. |
| `rr_block`, `round_robins` | Round robins alternate in blocks of this many neighbouring velocities (default 4; 1 for kits). |
| `onset_db`, `max_seconds`, `ring_db`, `envelope`, `attenuation_db` | Silence trim threshold, a cut with a 50 ms fade, a per-take cut where the ring last reaches `ring_db` under its own peak (smoothed over 50 ms; `max_seconds` still caps it), `attack`/`hold`/`decay`/`release` in seconds, `sustain_db`, `filter_hz`. Kits use a long release (100 s) so a short note does not cut a cymbal. |
| `exclusive_class` | Zones that cut each other, such as closed, pedal and open hi-hat. |
| `velocity_attenuation_cb`, `velocity_curve`, `velocity_filter_cents` | Replace the synthesizer's velocity curves (960 cB concave; −2400 cents of filter) when recorded layers already carry the dynamics. With `velocity_curve: "linear"` the attenuation falls on a straight line in dB, the same step per velocity value; 0 cB makes velocity change nothing, as on an organ. |

A preset with `"level_reference": "preset"` measures one reference over all its layers, so a kit keeps the recorded balance between kick, snare and cymbals; if the loudest hit would clip, the whole kit comes down together.

Recordings of notes played one at a time, one dynamic per file, are cut into notes first: a spec's `split` section lists each source file with its dynamic and the run it holds (`"notes": ["C4", "B4"]`), and optionally its `string` with the group's preferred `strings` ranges, `partial` where a file may lack notes, `whole` for a file that is one note (`pitch: "spectrum"` checks struck metal by its strongest partial), `out` to name an unpitched one. `soundbank.py split <spec> --root <sources> --output <notes>` writes one WAV per note and `split.json`, which records every cut, its pitch and its hash; `soundbank.py cut <split.json> --root <sources> --output <notes>` cuts the same notes again and checks each hash, so an install reproduces the intake exactly. Then build from the notes folder.

Before writing a part around a new bank, render a short probe (velocity sweep, the figure you intend, the register you need), measure attack, range and level, and get a human audition of a dry reel. A bank that renders without errors is not yet shown to be good.
